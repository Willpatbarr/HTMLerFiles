Important

Ignore This — It's a Measurement Artifact

The bundle contains two lsregister high-CPU samples that look alarming. They are sysdiagnose's own doing — the tool dumps the LaunchServices database as part of collection.

Don't chase this one. It is not a real problem on your machine.

Must Know

The Fix Plan, In Order

Order matters. Freeing the disk first is the highest leverage and partly relieves the memory pressure too.

Step 1 — See what simulator runtimes you have

xcrun simctl runtime list

Step 2 — Delete the ones you don't need

xcrun simctl runtime delete <identifier>

Biggest single win available. Target roughly 100 GB.

Step 3 — Clear Xcode build cache

rm -rf ~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData/*

Step 4 — Reboot

Clears the compressor and 41 days of accumulated state.

Step 5 — Turn off Apple Intelligence

System Settings, if you don't actually use it. On an 8 GB M1 it buys very little and costs a lot of CPU, RAM, and battery.

Remember Only 3 Things

  1. The hardware is fine. Thermals nominal, battery healthy, no crashes. Nobody needs a repair shop.
  2. The disk is the root cause. 97% full, and ~114 GB of it is simulator runtimes you don't use.
  3. 8 GB is the real ceiling. After the cleanup, if it still drags, that's the constraint — not a bug.

What To Do Next

  1. Run xcrun simctl runtime list and delete every runtime you're not actively testing against.
  2. Clear DerivedData, then confirm you've recovered 90–100 GB of free space.
  3. Reboot, then decide whether to disable Apple Intelligence based on whether you actually use it.
